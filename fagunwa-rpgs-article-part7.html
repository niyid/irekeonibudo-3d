📅 September 2026 · 🔗 github.com/niyid/ogbojuode-3d · irekeonibudo-3d · 📸 20 screenshots, 4 duels per kingdom · ⏱ 6 min read

This entry has now been checked against its own screenshots twice. The first pass mixed establishing and mood shots in with the fights; the redo dropped those and kept only in-duel captures, which is when two of the eight creatures — Ejá Jagunjagun and Arogidigba — turned out not to visually match their names. Five files have since been re-rendered: ireke_05 through ireke_09, the same two encounters. Comparing the new files to the old ones byte-for-byte shows exactly what changed — both creatures were pulled out from inside the tree canopy into open space, which is a camera/staging fix, not a model fix. That happens to be enough to make one of the two read correctly, and not enough for the other. Details below, per shot.

Redo

The First Twenty Weren't Wrong, Just Off-Target

The previous version of this entry proved the wizard's Build Entire Universe command puts an actual scene on screen — that part held up. What it didn't do was hold every image to the same standard: some shots were framing (establishing, mood, "full pack"), which is atmosphere, not evidence. This pass drops all of that and keeps only the twenty frames where the player is actually squared off against a creature, one duel at a time, four duels per kingdom.

🏷️
The caption bar is real, not added after

Each PNG in screenshots/ already has a title and one line of description burned into the bottom of the frame by the capture tool itself — IREKE ONIBUDO — Ireke vs. Ejo Fifo, for example. Nothing below re-describes what that bar already says; the prose under each image adds what the caption doesn't cover — angle, what's actually legible in the model, what looks unfinished.

One Structure, Two Skins

Four Duels, Same Order, Both Kingdoms

Both projects walk the player through the same four-encounter shape — a fast opening threat, a slower brute, a towering guard, and a riddle boss at the end — reflavored per story. The models themselves are not shared meshes in a costume; each creature is its own sculpt from the Tripo/Meshy prompt list. What's shared is the encounter order and, on the forest side at least, language the game's own captions use to describe it — "fast, fragile" for the opener, "slow, brutal" for the second.

🧱
One fix landed, one didn't — and the byte diff shows why

Five files were re-rendered for this revision (ireke_05 through ireke_09), and checking them against the previous upload byte-for-byte confirms exactly what changed: the camera and tree placement moved so neither creature is occluded by canopy foliage anymore. That's a staging fix, not a model fix — and it landed differently on the two creatures it touched. Arogidigba now reads clearly: golden head, a pale fin or collar shape at the chest, a tail fading from white to teal. Ejá Jagunjagun is unchanged underneath — still a symmetrical spread of arms around a thin spear, no head or torso of its own — it's just easier to see that now that it isn't half-buried in a tree. Getting a clear look at an unfinished model is progress, but it isn't the same as the model being finished.

Conclusion

The Same Rule, Applied a Third Time

Two flagged creatures came back as one fix and one non-fix, and a byte-level diff of the re-uploaded files is what made the difference legible: both re-renders were the same staging change — creature moved clear of the tree — and that change was enough to complete Arogidigba's design and not enough to touch Ejá Jagunjagun's. The honest version of this update is a downgrade from two open issues to one, not a clean bill. The next entry that owes the series a compile check and a riddle UI still owes them, and now it owes a pass at giving Ejá Jagunjagun an actual head and body rather than a wider view of the one it already has.
